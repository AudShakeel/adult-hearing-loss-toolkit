For Clinicians

Using this toolkit in adult aural rehabilitation

This guide supports counseling, education, home practice, follow-up planning, and family communication support.

Suggested Clinical Use by Appointment Type

Appointment typeSuggested toolkit use
Adult diagnostic evaluationUse Articles 1-3 to explain hearing loss, audiogram findings, and daily-life impact.
Audiogram counselingUse Article 2 and Workbook 2 to connect thresholds, speech clarity, and patient goals.
Hearing aid evaluationUse Article 4 to discuss what hearing aids can and cannot do, plus realistic expectations.
Hearing aid fittingUse Article 5 and Workbook 5 to prepare the patient for first-month adaptation and follow-up feedback.
Hearing aid follow-upReview Workbook 5, address sound-quality concerns, and add Article 8 or 9 as needed.
Communication strategy counselingUse Article 6 and Workbook 6 for self-advocacy and repair strategies.
Family counselingUse Article 7, Workbook 7, and the Family & Friends page.
Assistive technology counselingUse Article 9 to discuss captions, remote microphones, TV tools, alerting devices, and public systems.
Emotional adjustment counselingUse Article 11 and Workbook 11 to normalize emotional reactions and support coping.
Long-term care planningUse Article 12 and Workbook 12 to create a patient-centered follow-up plan.

5-Minute Counseling Workflow

  1. Identify the patient's main listening concern.
  2. Select one matching article and one workbook page.
  3. Explain the key message in plain language.
  4. Choose one strategy or action step with the patient.
  5. Review the workbook response at the next visit.

How to Use Workbooks for Follow-Up

Workbooks help patients document real-life listening experiences between appointments. At follow-up, clinicians can review which situations improved, which remained difficult, and which technology, communication, or counseling adjustments are needed.

Example Patient Journey

A newly diagnosed adult begins with Article 1 and Workbook 1 to identify listening challenges. During audiogram counseling, Article 2 helps connect thresholds to daily communication. If hearing aids are recommended, Articles 4 and 5 support expectations and first-month adaptation. If background noise remains difficult, Article 8 and Article 9 introduce strategies and technology. If family communication is affected, Article 7 guides communication partner support. Article 12 helps the patient build a long-term hearing care plan.
