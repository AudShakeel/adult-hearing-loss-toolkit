Glossary

Patient-friendly definitions

Audiogram

A graph that shows the softest sounds a person can hear at different pitches.

Frequency

Pitch. Low sounds are on the left side of an audiogram and high sounds are on the right.

Decibel

A measure of loudness.

Threshold

The softest sound a person responds to during testing.

Word Recognition

A speech test that shows how clearly a person understands words when they are loud enough.

Conductive Hearing Loss

Hearing loss related to the outer ear or middle ear.

Sensorineural Hearing Loss

Hearing loss related to the inner ear or hearing nerve pathway.

Mixed Hearing Loss

A hearing loss with both conductive and sensorineural parts.

Listening Fatigue

Tiredness that can happen when listening takes extra effort.

Aural Rehabilitation

Education, counseling, technology, and strategies that help people manage hearing loss.

Assistive Listening Device

A device that helps improve access to speech or important sounds.

Remote Microphone

A microphone placed near a speaker that sends the voice closer to the listener.

Telecoil

A hearing aid feature that can connect with hearing loop systems.

Hearing Loop

A public listening system that sends sound to telecoil-enabled hearing devices.

Captions

Written words that show spoken information.

Communication Partner

A person who communicates regularly with someone who has hearing loss.

Hearing Aid

A device that makes selected sounds easier to hear.

Otc Hearing Aid

An over-the-counter hearing aid for some adults with perceived mild to moderate hearing loss.

Prescription Hearing Aid

A hearing aid selected, fit, and programmed by a hearing care professional.

Auditory Fatigue

Tiredness related to listening effort.
