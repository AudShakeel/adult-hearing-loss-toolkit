Know the clues

Signs and Next Steps

Hearing loss can be gradual, so people often notice the effects before they notice the hearing change. This page helps adults decide when it is time to get checked.

Do not wait for urgent symptoms

Seek prompt medical care if hearing changes suddenly, especially in one ear. Also seek care for sudden ringing, ear pain, drainage, dizziness, vertigo, sudden ear pressure, facial weakness, or new one-sided hearing difficulty.

Teacher note: If hearing drops suddenly, treat it like a smoke alarm. Do not cover it and hope it stops. Get help quickly.

Everyday signs

Common clues that hearing may be working harder than it should

  • Voices are harder to understand in restaurants, cars, meetings, or family gatherings.
  • People sound like they are mumbling.
  • You hear sound but miss the words.
  • You ask people to repeat often.
  • You miss names, numbers, medication instructions, or appointment details.
  • Other people say the TV, radio, or phone is too loud.
  • Phone calls and video calls feel harder than face-to-face conversations.
  • You avoid social events because listening feels tiring or embarrassing.
  • You feel left out even when you are physically in the room.
  • You hear ringing, buzzing, hissing, or roaring sounds that others do not hear.

The listening battery

Listening in noise is like keeping too many apps open on your phone. The battery drains faster. When hearing is reduced, the brain spends extra energy filling in missing speech pieces.

Simple memory tool

Use the MAP plan

When hearing feels harder, use MAP to decide what to do next.

M

Monitor the moment

Notice where the problem happens. Is it noise, distance, masks, phone calls, fast speech, or poor lighting?

A

Ask for access

Ask for a quieter seat, written instructions, captions, a microphone, or a slower pace. Access is not being difficult.

P

Plan an evaluation

If the problem keeps showing up, schedule a comprehensive hearing evaluation with an audiologist.

What happens at a comprehensive audiology evaluation?

An audiology visit is more than raising your hand when you hear beeps. It can include a conversation about your listening goals, ear health questions, pure-tone testing, speech testing, middle ear testing, and counseling about next steps.

Part of the visitWhat it helps answer
Case historyWhere are you struggling? What matters most to you? What medical or noise history may matter?
OtoscopyDoes the ear canal look clear? Is there wax, irritation, or something that needs referral?
Pure-tone testingWhat is the softest sound you can hear at different pitches?
Speech testingHow clearly do you understand speech in quiet or sometimes with noise?
Middle ear testingIs the eardrum and middle ear system moving as expected?
Counseling and planDo you need hearing aids, assistive technology, medical referral, communication strategies, or monitoring?

Before your appointment

Bring your real-life listening examples

A hearing test is most helpful when the audiologist knows your daily life. Bring three examples such as:

  • "I miss my spouse from the kitchen."
  • "I avoid restaurants."
  • "I hear my doctor but miss medication instructions."
  • "I turn on captions even when the volume is high."

One sentence to use

"I am not sure if I have hearing loss, but communication is getting harder and I would like a full audiological evaluation."
