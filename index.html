What this toolkit does

A practical guide for real life, not just a pamphlet

Hearing difficulty can show up at dinner, on the phone, at work, in worship services, during medical appointments, or while watching TV with family. This toolkit turns hearing health information into simple steps people can actually use.

01

Notice the clues

Learn the everyday signs that hearing may need attention, such as missing words in noise, asking for repeats, raising the TV volume, or avoiding group conversations.

02

Try better strategies

Use simple communication tools like clear speech, good lighting, captions, remote microphones, and repair phrases that keep conversations moving.

03

Know the next step

When signs are present, the best first step is a comprehensive audiological evaluation. A hearing aid purchase is not the same as a diagnosis.

Do I need a hearing check?

Common signs that hearing may be affecting daily life

You may want an evaluation if several of these feel familiar:

  • Speech sounds clear until background noise is added.
  • People seem to mumble, even when they say they are speaking normally.
  • You ask others to repeat often.
  • The TV or phone volume feels normal to you but loud to someone else.
  • Phone calls and video calls feel harder than face-to-face conversations.
  • You avoid restaurants, meetings, family gatherings, or worship services because listening feels tiring.
  • You hear ringing, buzzing, hissing, or roaring sounds that others do not hear.

Quick analogy

Speech is like a puzzle. Hearing loss can remove small pieces from the puzzle. Your brain may still guess the picture, but guessing all day can be exhausting.

Start the Hearing Check

Audiologist first

Get the hearing map before choosing the tool

An audiologist can evaluate the type, degree, and pattern of hearing loss, speech understanding, middle ear function, listening goals, and whether medical referral is needed. A retail hearing center or hearing aid dispenser may be helpful later for device access, but a device purchase is not the same as a diagnostic evaluation.

Think of the audiogram as the map. A hearing aid, captioning app, remote microphone, or cochlear implant evaluation may be one part of the travel plan. Start with the map.

Communication tools

The BRIGHT plan

Use this simple memory tool when conversations feel hard.

BBring the voice closer.
RReduce background noise.
IInclude a communication partner.
GGet the right tool.
HHave a repair phrase ready.
TTest with an audiologist.

Clinic friendly

Easy to use in a clinic, classroom, library, or health fair

  1. Open the Hearing Check on a tablet or computer.
  2. Ask the person to choose one difficult listening situation.
  3. Teach one strategy from the BRIGHT plan.
  4. Review urgent warning signs.
  5. Recommend an audiologist when the answers suggest hearing difficulty.

Small change, big difference

Moving closer to the speaker, adding captions, choosing a quieter table, or asking for written instructions can turn a stressful moment into a manageable one.
